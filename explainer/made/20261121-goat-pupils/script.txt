Rectangle pupils let your goat see 320 degrees,
almost behind its own head.
Why rectangles?
Goats are on the menu for wolves and big cats,
so they need to spot danger from almost any direction.
A long sideways slit stretches their view out wide,
right along the ground,
where predators actually sneak up.
With eyes on the sides of the head,
only a thin blind spot is left behind it.
The slit also blocks glare from the bright sky.
Now here's the weird part.
When a goat drops its head
to eat grass,
its eyeballs roll the other way,
about 50 degrees each,
so the slits stay level with the horizon.
You can only do that a few degrees.
So would you swap your round pupils,
for a goat's rectangles?
