How does a plane toilet flush
at thirty-five thousand feet?
It barely uses water.
It uses the sky.
Your cabin is pressurized,
packed with air like a balloon.
Outside, the air is super thin.
Press the button,
and a valve opens a pipe
from the bowl to that thin outside air.
The high pressure in the cabin
shoves everything down the pipe.
That's the whoosh.
It all lands in a tank at the back of the plane.
About three seconds later,
the valve slams shut.
But below sixteen thousand feet,
the outside air isn't thin enough,
so an electric pump makes the vacuum instead.
And no, nothing gets dumped in the sky.
Ground crews empty the tank after landing.
So, next flight,
are you brave enough to press it?
