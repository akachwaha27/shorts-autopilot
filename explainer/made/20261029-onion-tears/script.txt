Onions fire tear gas at your eyes,
30 seconds after you cut them.
How?
Inside every onion cell,
two chemicals are kept apart,
in separate little rooms.
One is a sulfur molecule.
The other is an enzyme.
Your knife smashes the walls,
and they finally mix.
The enzyme turns the sulfur into an acid,
then a second enzyme,
only discovered in 2002,
flips that acid into a gas.
It drifts up,
and peaks about 30 seconds after the cut.
When it hits your eyes,
tiny nerves shout "burning!"
So your tear glands flood your eyes,
to wash it away.
Why bother?
It's armor.
Any animal that bites an onion,
gets a face full of sting.
Want fewer tears?
Cut it under running water.
So, would you still eat a plant that fights back with tear gas?
