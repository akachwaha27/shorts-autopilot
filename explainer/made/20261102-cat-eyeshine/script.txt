Cat eyes never glow,
that shine is your own flashlight bouncing back at you.
Light comes in through the pupil,
and hits the retina at the back of the eye.
Tiny cells catch it,
and send a picture to the brain.
But in your eye,
some light slips right past them,
and it's gone.
A cat gets a second chance.
Behind its retina sits a shiny layer,
called the tapetum lucidum.
It works like a mirror,
bouncing missed light back through the retina.
So a cat needs just one-sixth of the light you do.
Whatever still isn't caught,
shoots back out of the eye.
That's the shine,
often green or yellow.
No light at all?
No shine, and no sight either.
Ever caught your cat's eyes shining at night?
What color were they?
