Your smoke alarm might be radioactive.
Nobody tells you,
but that's how it smells smoke.
Inside sits a speck of americium,
which fires out tiny alpha particles.
They slam into the air
between two metal plates,
knocking electrons loose.
Now the air carries electricity,
so a tiny, steady current flows.
When smoke drifts in,
bits of smoke grab those charged particles.
The current drops,
and the alarm screams.
Other alarms use light instead.
An LED shines across a dark chamber,
away from a sensor.
No smoke, no light hits it.
But smoke scatters the beam,
bouncing light into the sensor.
Beep!
And that americium?
Its half-life is four hundred thirty-two years,
so it outlasts your alarm by centuries.
So, is the one above your bed
radioactive, or running on light?
