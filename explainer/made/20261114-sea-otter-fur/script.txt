A sea otter has no blubber,
but you'd freeze long before it would. Why?
Whales and seals wrap up in thick fat.
The otter's secret is fur.
Up to a million hairs on every square inch,
the densest fur of any animal.
That's more hairs on a stamp-sized patch
than you have on your whole head.
Those hairs trap a layer of air,
so cold water never touches its skin.
That's why otters groom for hours,
fluffing air into their coats.
But fur alone isn't enough.
Its muscles leak energy as heat,
like an engine idling all day.
So it burns fuel about three times faster
than other animals its size.
To keep up, it eats about a quarter of its body weight
every single day.
Would you trade your jacket for a million hairs,
and a quarter of your weight in snacks?
