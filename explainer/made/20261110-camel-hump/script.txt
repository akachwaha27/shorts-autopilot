A camel's hump hides up to 35 kilos of fat,
not a drop of water.
Here's why you never knew.
Fat is food in a backpack.
When there's nothing to eat,
the camel burns that fat for energy,
and the hump shrinks and flops over.
With all the fat in one lump,
the rest of the body stays lean and cool.
Okay, so where's the water?
It's spread through the whole body.
A camel can lose a quarter of its weight in water
and keep walking.
Its oval blood cells keep sliding along
even as its blood thickens.
Then it finds a well,
and drinks around a hundred litres
in about ten minutes.
Those blood cells swell to twice their size
without bursting.
So if you had a hump,
what would you store in yours?
