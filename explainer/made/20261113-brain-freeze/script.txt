Ice cream gives you a 30-second headache,
but your brain never freezes.
So what's going on?
It starts on the roof of your mouth.
It's packed with nerves and blood vessels,
and ice cream chills it super fast.
Scientists think a big artery behind your eyes
suddenly opens wide,
to rush in warm blood.
That flood of blood
pushes up the pressure inside your head.
Your trigeminal nerve feels it,
but it gets mixed up,
and sends the pain to your forehead instead.
That's called referred pain.
Once things warm up,
the artery narrows again,
and the ache fades, usually in under 30 seconds.
Want it gone faster?
Press your warm tongue
to the roof of your mouth.
