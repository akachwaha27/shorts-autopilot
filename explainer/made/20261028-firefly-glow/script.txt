Fireflies make cold light.
Your old bulb wastes ninety percent as heat,
so how do fireflies stay cool?
Inside a firefly's tail,
sits a glowing organ called the lantern.
Its cells are packed with a fuel, luciferin,
and an enzyme, luciferase.
Add a spark of energy,
and a puff of oxygen,
and the fuel lights up.
No fire, just chemistry.
But how does it blink?
The firefly controls the oxygen.
A gas called nitric oxide opens the gate,
air rushes in,
flash!
Close the gate, and it goes dark.
And every flash is a message.
There are over two thousand kinds of fireflies,
and each one blinks its own code.
Males flash as they fly,
and females answer back.
So, if you could flash one message in the dark, what would it say?
