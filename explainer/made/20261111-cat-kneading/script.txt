Why does your cat knead you?
It's a kitten trick it never quit.
Newborn kittens can barely see,
but they can push.
They press tiny front paws into their mom,
left, right, left,
and that gets the milk flowing.
Push, push, sip.
Most cats just never outgrow it.
Grown cats knead when they feel safe and calm,
so a cat kneading your lap
is kind of a big compliment.
But there's a sneaky second job.
Cats have scent glands between their paw pads.
Every push leaves a smell you can't notice,
but other cats can.
It's like a tiny stamp that says, mine.
Wild cats even knead soft grass
to make a comfy bed before a nap.
So when your cat kneads you,
is it being a kitten again,
or stamping you as its property?
