This shark will lose thirty thousand teeth.
So why does it never run out?
Look inside its jaw.
Behind the front teeth,
there are rows and rows of spares,
folded back against the inside of the jaw.
Most sharks carry about fifteen rows.
Your teeth are locked into bone.
A shark's teeth sit in soft tissue,
so they pop out easily.
When one snaps off,
the tooth behind it swings up,
and takes its place,
sometimes in just one day.
Meanwhile, a brand new tooth
starts growing at the very back.
It's like a moving staircase of teeth,
always rolling forward.
A great white goes through
about twenty thousand teeth in its life.
And this lemon shark?
Scientists think more than thirty thousand.
