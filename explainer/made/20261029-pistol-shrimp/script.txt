A shrimp smaller than your finger,
fires a bubble almost as hot as the Sun.
But how?
Meet the pistol shrimp.
One claw is huge,
and it cocks open like a gun's hammer.
When it snaps shut,
a plug slams into a socket,
and fires a jet of water,
at about ninety kilometres an hour.
That jet is so fast,
the water behind it rips apart,
into a tiny bubble.
Then the bubble collapses,
in less than a thousandth of a second.
Inside, it hits about four thousand seven hundred degrees,
nearly as hot as the Sun's surface.
It even flashes light.
And that bang isn't the claw.
It's the bubble popping,
one of the loudest sounds in the ocean,
loud enough to stun a passing fish.
So what animal could beat this shrimp's snap?
