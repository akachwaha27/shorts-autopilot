You lose one centimeter of height every day,
but why?
Your spine isn't one solid bone.
It's a stack of vertebrae,
with twenty-three squishy discs in between.
Each disc is like a jelly doughnut,
with a soft, watery middle.
All day, gravity pushes down on you.
Every step and every sit
squeezes a little water out,
and the discs flatten a tiny bit.
Add up all twenty-three,
and you've shrunk about a centimeter by bedtime.
But when you lie down to sleep,
the weight comes off your spine.
The discs soak water back up,
like little sponges,
and by morning, you're tall again.
In space, with no gravity,
astronauts can grow three percent taller!
So tonight, measure yourself before bed,
then again when you wake up.
Will you be one centimeter taller tomorrow morning?
