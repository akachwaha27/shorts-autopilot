Your knuckle crack is actually a gas bubble,
and it can't happen again for twenty minutes.
Every finger joint sits in a tiny capsule,
filled with a slippery fluid.
That fluid has gas dissolved in it,
like a soda with the cap on.
When you pull or bend the joint,
the capsule stretches,
and the pressure inside drops.
The gas rushes out of the fluid,
and a bubble forms, with a pop!
In 2015, scientists filmed it in an MRI scanner,
and saw the bubble appear right at the crack.
Try that knuckle again, and... nothing.
The bubble needs about twenty minutes
to dissolve back into the fluid.
And no, studies say it won't give you arthritis.
One doctor cracked only his left hand's knuckles
for sixty years,
and both hands stayed the same.
So, how many bubbles have you made today?
