Lobsters never stop growing.
So why don't you see 40-pound ones?
First, a lobster lives inside hard armor.
To get bigger, it climbs right out of it.
The old shell splits open across the back
and the lobster wriggles free,
claws, legs and all.
Then it gulps seawater and swells up
before the new soft shell hardens.
A young lobster molts about 25 times
in its first few years.
And deep in its cells
an enzyme called telomerase
keeps repairing their DNA,
so the cells keep dividing.
That's how one lobster in Nova Scotia
grew to 20 kilograms.
That's 44 pounds!
But here's the catch.
Every molt costs more energy.
Some old lobsters die of exhaustion mid-molt.
Others just stop molting,
stuck in a worn-out shell.
So would you want to keep growing forever?
