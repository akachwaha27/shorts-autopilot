Giraffe tongues are almost black,
and you'd never guess why.
They can spend up to twelve hours a day out in the sun.
That tongue is forty-five to fifty centimeters long,
longer than your whole forearm.
It wraps thorny acacia branches like a hand,
and tough little bumps called papillae
armor it against the spikes.
But all that eating happens in the African sun.
So scientists think the dark color is built-in sunscreen.
The front, the part that sticks out,
is packed with dark pigment.
The base, tucked inside the mouth,
is plain pink.
And honestly, that's kind of genius.
But it's not proven.
The okapi, the giraffe's forest cousin,
has the same dark tongue in the shade.
So why is a giraffe's tongue almost black?
Sunscreen, or something else?
