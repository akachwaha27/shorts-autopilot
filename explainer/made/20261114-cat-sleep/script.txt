Your cat sleeps 16 hours a day,
but it's almost never fully asleep.
So why all the napping?
Cats are hunters built for short explosive bursts.
Stalking and pouncing burn a ton of energy in seconds,
and plenty of pounces miss.
So between hunts a cat powers down
to save fuel for the next one.
It's most awake at dawn and dusk,
when mice are on the move.
That's why yours goes wild at 5 a.m.
Most of its sleep is light dozing,
in naps of 15 to 30 minutes,
senses still switched on.
One tiny squeak, and it's awake.
Only about a quarter is real deep sleep,
in bursts of 5 to 7 minutes.
So your cat isn't lazy.
It's charging up for the next sprint.
So is your cat asleep right now, or just waiting?
