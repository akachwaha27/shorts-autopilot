Your cat's purr is a twitch,
twenty-five times a second.
But how?
Deep in the throat sits the voice box,
with a tiny gap called the glottis.
A signal from the brain
makes the muscles around it twitch,
snapping that gap open and shut.
Air rushes in,
and the gap chops it into bursts.
Air flows out,
and it chops it again.
That's why a purr just keeps going.
In twenty twenty-three, scientists found another clue:
tiny pads inside the vocal folds,
which may help a small cat hit such a low note.
Bobcats and cheetahs purr too,
but lions and tigers don't seem to.
And a purr doesn't always mean happy.
Cats also purr at the vet,
or when they're hurt.
So when yours starts twitching away,
do you think it's happy, or worried?
