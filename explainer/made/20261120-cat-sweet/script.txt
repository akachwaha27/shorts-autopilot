Your cat can't taste sugar,
and one broken gene is why.
Put a sweet treat in front of one,
and to the cat, it tastes like nothing.
To taste sweet, your tongue needs a tiny lock,
built from two parts that work as a pair.
In 2005, scientists looked inside cat DNA,
and found one of those parts was broken.
The gene for it is missing 247 letters,
so the lock never gets built.
No lock, no sweet.
And it's not just house cats.
Lions, tigers and cheetahs
carry the same broken gene.
Which honestly makes sense.
Cats eat almost nothing but meat,
and meat has barely any sugar.
So losing sweet cost them nothing at all.
So if your cat keeps stealing your ice cream,
what do you think it's really after?
