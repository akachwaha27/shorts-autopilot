A dead flamingo can stand on one leg.
Why can't you?
In 2017, scientists in Atlanta tested it.
They propped up a flamingo body,
and it balanced on one leg,
with no muscles working at all.
Here's the trick.
That bendy joint isn't a knee.
It's the ankle.
The real knee is hidden up in the feathers.
When a flamingo lifts one leg,
it shifts its body,
so its weight sits right over the foot.
That weight pushes the joints into a locked position,
like a folding chair clicking open.
No muscles needed, just gravity.
Live flamingos even sway less
when they're fast asleep.
But on two legs,
the dead bird couldn't stay up.
For a flamingo, one leg is the lazy way to stand.
For you, it's a muscle workout.
So how long can you stand on one leg?
