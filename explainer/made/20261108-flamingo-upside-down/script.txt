You've never seen a flamingo eat right-side up.
It feeds with its head upside down,
because its beak is built backwards.
Most birds have a big top beak,
and a smaller bottom one.
A flamingo's is the other way round.
So it flips its head over,
and the big lower beak skims the water like a scoop.
Inside, the edges are lined with tiny comb-like plates.
Then the tongue starts pumping,
about four or five times a second,
like a little piston.
Water squirts out through the combs,
and tiny shrimp and algae get trapped inside.
Flamingos even stomp in the mud,
to stir up little whirlpools full of food.
And those shrimp and algae?
They're packed with pink pigments.
That's what turns a flamingo pink.
So, would you eat upside down,
