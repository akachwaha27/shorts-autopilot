Your skin hides five million tiny muscles. Why?
Almost every hair on your body
has its own muscle,
called the arrector pili.
When you get cold,
or scared,
your nerves fire one signal,
and they all squeeze at once.
That yanks each hair upright,
and pushes up a bump of skin.
That's a goosebump.
For a furry animal, it's a superpower.
Standing fur traps a layer of warm air,
like a puffy winter coat.
And a scared cat puffs up,
so it looks bigger.
But we lost most of our fur,
so now we just get bumps.
Even a song can trigger them,
because big feelings fire the same nerves.
And in mice, scientists found
those nerves wake up hair stem cells,
to grow thicker fur in the cold.
So what gives you goosebumps?
