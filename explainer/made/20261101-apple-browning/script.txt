Why does your apple grow a brown scab in 5 minutes?
Inside every apple cell,
two things are locked in separate rooms.
One holds an enzyme, polyphenol oxidase.
The other holds natural chemicals called polyphenols.
When you bite or slice the apple,
you burst thousands of cells.
The two finally meet,
and oxygen from the air rushes in.
The enzyme uses that oxygen
to turn the polyphenols into brown pigment,
like the melanin in your skin.
Scientists think it's a defence,
a brown shield against germs and bugs,
just like a scab on your knee.
Want to stop it?
Squeeze on some lemon juice.
Its acid slows the enzyme down,
and its vitamin C grabs the oxygen first.
So when your apple turns brown,
is it rotting, or healing?
