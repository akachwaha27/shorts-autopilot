You go blind fifteen thousand times every day.
Why can't you tell?
It's called blinking.
Each blink lasts about a tenth of a second.
The clear front of your eye has no blood vessels,
so it relies on a film of tears instead.
That film has three parts:
mucus, water, and oil.
Every blink wipes on a fresh coat,
like a tiny windshield wiper,
and sweeps dust toward your eye's drain.
So why doesn't the world go black?
Scientists scanned people's brains during blinks,
and found parts of the visual system go quiet,
right as your eyelid comes down.
Your brain deletes the blackout,
before you can ever see it.
Altogether, your eyes are shut for about half an hour every day.
So how many seconds can you go without blinking?
Tell me your record.
