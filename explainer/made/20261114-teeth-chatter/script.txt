Your chattering teeth mean your brain is burning fuel before you freeze.
When your skin senses cold,
a spot deep in your brain called the hypothalamus
hits the panic button.
It can't just crank up a thermostat,
so it makes your muscles shiver.
Tiny fast squeezes, over and over,
and every squeeze burns fuel and leaks heat.
At full blast, shivering can make about five times
the heat your body makes at rest.
Your jaw muscles join in too.
But your jaw is small and light,
and it hangs on a hinge,
so every twitch swings it.
Top teeth smack bottom teeth.
Click, click, click.
Your legs and back are shivering too,
they're just too heavy to rattle.
And honestly, that's kind of genius.
Your body turns its own muscles into a furnace.
So be honest.
When you're freezing, what chatters first, your teeth or your knees?
