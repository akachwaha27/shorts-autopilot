How does a thermos keep coffee hot
for twelve whole hours?
Heat escapes in three ways.
It can touch its way out,
that's conduction.
It can ride on moving air,
that's convection.
Or it can glow away as invisible light,
called radiation.
A thermos blocks all three.
It's really two bottles,
one inside the other,
and the gap between them is a vacuum.
With no air in there,
heat has nothing to touch,
and nothing to ride.
Then the walls are shiny,
like a mirror,
so the glow bounces back into your coffee.
A scientist named James Dewar
invented it in 1892,
to keep liquid gases ice cold.
The only real leak?
The lid.
