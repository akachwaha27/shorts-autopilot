Close or far, it doesn't matter,
it's always roughly seven.
It copies their speed and their turns,
and keeps a tiny gap.
So when one bird turns,
its seven neighbours turn too,
then their neighbours,
and the turn ripples through the flock like a wave.
Scientists found that wave travels
about three times faster than a single bird can react.
Each bird sees it coming,
like dancers in a chorus line.
And seven seems to be the sweet spot,
enough to stay together,
without watching everyone at once.
So could you dodge ten thousand people
watching just seven?
