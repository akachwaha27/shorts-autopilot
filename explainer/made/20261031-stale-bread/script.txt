Your fridge is wasting your bread.
It can go stale six times faster.
But why?
Fresh bread is soft because of starch.
In the oven, starch soaks up water,
and its tight crystals melt into a soft gel.
But once the bread cools,
the starch packs back into crystals.
Scientists call it retrogradation.
Those crystals squeeze the water out,
and lock it away.
So the crumb turns hard,
even though it's barely dried out.
That's why sealed bread still goes stale.
And this crystal building runs fastest
just above freezing,
right at fridge temperature.
In the freezer, it almost stops.
The fix?
Warm stale bread in the oven,
and the crystals melt again.
It goes soft, almost like fresh,
but only for a little while.
So, fridge, counter, or freezer:
where does your bread live?
