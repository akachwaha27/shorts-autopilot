Ostriches never bury their heads in sand,
but you've seen it happen. Why?
Blame a Roman writer, Pliny the Elder,
nearly two thousand years ago.
He wrote that ostriches hide their heads in bushes
and think nobody can see them.
Here's what's really going on.
Ostriches dig a shallow hole in the ground
and lay their giant eggs inside.
Several times a day, mom and dad dip their heads down
and roll each egg with their beak.
From far away, the head vanishes.
And if danger's close and they can't get away,
they flop flat, neck stretched along the dirt,
like a big lumpy bush.
But mostly? They just run.
Up to seventy kilometers an hour,
the fastest bird on two legs.
So next time an ostrich looks like it's hiding,
what's it really doing?
