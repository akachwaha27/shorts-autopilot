Your goldfish would freeze solid in this minus 1.9 degree water,
so how does this fish survive?
The sea around Antarctica stays liquid
because salt lowers its freezing point.
But normal fish blood freezes at about minus 0.7.
So a regular fish would turn into a fishsicle.
The trick is hiding in its blood.
Antarctic icefish and their cousins make antifreeze proteins.
When a tiny ice crystal sneaks inside,
the proteins grab onto its surface
and stop it from growing.
The crystal stays too small to do harm.
Scientists even found ice
in their spleens, all year round.
And the wildest part?
The antifreeze gene evolved from a gene
for a digestive enzyme.
Evolution recycled a gut gene into a winter coat.
So would you trade your blood for antifreeze
and swim in minus 1.9 degree water?
