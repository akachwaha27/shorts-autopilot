Your eyes slam shut every time you sneeze,
and you almost can't stop it. Why?
People say your eyeballs would pop out
if they stayed open.
That's a myth.
The pressure builds in your chest,
not behind your eyes.
So what's going on?
A tickle inside your nose
sends a nerve signal to your brainstem,
your sneeze control center.
It doesn't ask permission.
It fires a chain of muscles all at once.
Deep breath in,
chest squeezes,
throat snaps open,
and your eyelids get dragged along for the ride.
Then the blast.
A sneeze cloud can drift about eight meters.
That's twenty-six feet!
Some scientists think shut eyes
help keep the spray out.
A few people can sneeze with their eyes open,
and their eyes stay right where they are.
So next time you sneeze,
will yours stay shut?
