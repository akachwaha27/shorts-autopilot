A three and a half centimeter tube in your skull
is why your ears pop.
It's called the Eustachian tube,
and it links your middle ear
to the back of your throat.
Behind your eardrum sits a tiny pocket of air.
On the ground, it matches the air outside.
But a plane cabin is only pressurized
like an eight thousand foot mountain.
So as you climb, the outside pressure drops,
and the air behind your eardrum pushes out.
Your eardrum bulges like a balloon,
until the tube cracks open,
air puffs out, and pop!
Landing is trickier.
Now the cabin air squeezes in,
and that pressure pinches the tube shut.
So you swallow, or you yawn.
Tiny muscles yank the tube open,
air rushes in,
and your eardrum snaps back flat.
So next flight,
will you open that secret tube with a yawn,
or a swallow?
