Zebra stripes stop 3 out of 4 fly landings,
and you can see why.
In Africa, biting horseflies swarm grazing animals,
and their bites can spread nasty diseases.
So researchers in Britain filmed flies
buzzing around zebras and horses.
The flies flew up to both just as often.
But they landed on zebras less than a quarter as often.
So what's going on?
Near a horse, a fly slows down for a soft landing.
Near a zebra, the stripes seem to scramble its eyes,
so it never brakes,
and it bumps right off or zooms past.
Then they dressed horses in striped coats.
Flies kept missing the coats,
but still landed on their bare heads.
Honestly, that's kind of genius.
Camouflage? Keeping cool? The evidence for those is weaker.
So would you wear stripes to your next picnic?
