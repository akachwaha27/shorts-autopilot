A weed invented your Velcro,
in nineteen forty-one,
and almost nobody believed it.
A Swiss engineer, George de Mestral,
took his dog hiking in the Alps.
They came home covered in burrs,
the spiky seed heads of burdock.
Under a microscope,
every spike ended in a tiny hook,
and his clothes were full of tiny loops.
Hook meets loop,
and they grab on.
So he tried to copy it.
Cotton wore out too fast.
Nylon worked,
sewn under hot infrared light
into stiff little hooks.
It took him about ten years,
and the patent came in nineteen fifty-five.
He named it from two French words,
velours, for velvet,
and crochet, for hook.
Astronauts used it
to keep snack pouches from floating away.
So when your shoe straps rip open,
do you blame the weed, or the dog?
