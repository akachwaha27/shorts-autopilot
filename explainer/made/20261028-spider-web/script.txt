Spider glue almost never lets a fly go.
So why do you never see the spider stuck?
Secret one: most of the web isn't sticky.
The spokes are dry silk.
Only the spiral has beads of glue,
so the spider walks the spokes.
Secret two: its feet.
Web spiders have three claws per foot.
Insects get two.
The extra claw hooks a single thread,
like a tiny hand.
But here's the wild part.
Building one web,
a spider touches its own glue
hundreds of times.
Its legs are covered in branched hairs,
so the glue slides along them
and drips off.
Scientists washed spider legs,
and they stuck more.
So there's a non-stick coating, too.
A fly hits lots of glue at once.
The spider barely touches a drop.
So which trick would you steal:
the claws, the hairs, or the coating?
