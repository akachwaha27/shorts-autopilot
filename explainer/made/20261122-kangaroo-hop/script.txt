How do kangaroos hit 50 kilometres an hour with almost no extra energy,
when you'd be gasping?
As a red kangaroo hops faster,
its oxygen use barely changes.
So why hop instead of run?
It's all in the legs.
Each one has a huge Achilles tendon,
and it works like a pogo spring.
When the kangaroo lands, the tendon stretches,
storing energy.
Then it snaps back
and launches the next hop.
One bound can cover up to nine metres.
And there's a bonus.
Every bounce sloshes its gut up and down,
which pumps air in and out of its lungs.
So hopping helps it breathe too.
Going slow is the clumsy part.
Then it walks on five legs,
using its tail as the fifth.
So if you had pogo-spring legs,
would you ever walk again?
