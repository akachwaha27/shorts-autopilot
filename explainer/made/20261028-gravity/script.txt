You spin a thousand miles an hour, every day,
so why don't you fly off into space?
The answer is gravity,
and it's weirder than you think.
Let's explain it in simple words.
Here's the big idea.
Everything with mass pulls on everything else.
The Earth pulls on you,
your phone pulls on your coffee,
and you pull on the whole Earth too.
Isaac Newton wrote this down in 1687.
His rule has two parts.
More mass means a stronger pull,
and more distance means a weaker one.
Double the distance,
and the pull drops to just one quarter.
The Earth is huge,
about six trillion trillion kilograms,
so its pull on you wins easily.
That pull is what you feel as weight.
On the Moon, which has much less mass,
you'd weigh about one sixth as much.
So, back to the spin.
At the equator, you really are moving
at about a thousand miles an hour.
Spinning does try to fling you outward,
like water off a spinning bike wheel.
But Earth's gravity is about three hundred times stronger
than that fling.
So you stay put, held to the ground.
For you to float away,
Earth would have to spin about seventeen times faster.
A whole day would last
less than an hour and a half.
And you don't feel the spin at all,
because the ground, the air and you
all move together, smoothly,
like passengers on a calm plane.
Now here's the wild part.
The Moon is falling toward us,
right now, and it never stops.
Newton pictured a cannon on a mountain.
Fire a cannonball slowly,
and it curves down and hits the ground.
Fire it faster, and it lands farther away.
Fire it fast enough,
and the ground curves away beneath it
as fast as it falls.
It keeps falling, and keeps missing.
That's an orbit.
The Moon does exactly this,
falling around the Earth
about once every twenty-seven days.
The Moon pulls back on us, too.
Its gravity tugs on the oceans,
making a bulge of water
on the side facing the Moon,
and another on the far side.
As Earth turns through those bulges,
most beaches get two high tides a day.
And astronauts?
On the space station,
about two hundred fifty miles up,
gravity is still about ninety percent as strong.
They float because they're falling,
just like that cannonball,
racing sideways at seventeen thousand miles an hour.
They circle the whole Earth
every ninety minutes.
Here's the strangest fact of all.
Gravity is by far the weakest force in nature.
A tiny fridge magnet
can lift a paperclip
against the pull of the entire planet.
Gravity only wins
because it adds up, and never switches off.
So the next time you jump,
remember,
you pull the whole Earth up toward you,
just a tiny, tiny bit.
