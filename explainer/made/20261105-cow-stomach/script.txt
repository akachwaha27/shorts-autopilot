Cows burp up their lunch on purpose,
and you'd never guess why.
Grass is really hard to digest.
Its walls are made of cellulose,
and cows can't break that down alone.
So one stomach has four rooms.
Room one is the rumen,
a vat holding up to forty gallons.
It's packed with tiny microbes,
and they ferment the grass.
Room two, the honeycomb, sorts the pieces.
Big chunks go back up her throat,
as a soggy ball called cud.
She chews it again,
for about eight hours a day.
Fine mush slides into room three,
where water gets squeezed out.
Room four is the true stomach,
full of acid, like yours.
All that fermenting makes gas,
hundreds of litres of methane a day,
and she burps it out.
So, would you chew your lunch twice?
