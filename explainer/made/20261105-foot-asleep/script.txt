Why does your foot lose signal when you sit on it?
Most people blame blood flow,
but scientists call that mostly a myth.
The real culprit is a nerve.
Think of it like a garden hose,
carrying signals instead of water.
Messages run from your foot to your brain.
So you cross your legs.
That pressure pinches the nerve,
like stepping on the hose.
Signals get blocked, or come through scrambled.
Your brain hears almost nothing,
so your foot goes numb.
Then you stand up.
The nerve starts working again,
but it isn't smooth yet.
Messy signals rush through,
and your brain probably reads them as tiny prickles.
Those are pins and needles,
and they usually fade in minutes.
So which part of you loses signal first:
your foot, your hand, or your arm?
