Hummingbirds almost shut down every night,
and you never notice.
Why would a heart drop from twelve hundred beats to fifty?
Their tiny engine burns fuel so fast
they can sip three times their weight in nectar a day.
But at night, there's no nectar.
A long night without food could starve them.
So they drop into torpor.
Their body cools from a hundred and five degrees
by almost fifty.
Their heartbeat crashes to around fifty a minute.
Some even hang upside down, stiff as a statue.
One Andes species chills to just thirty-eight degrees Fahrenheit,
barely above freezing.
It slows their metabolism by up to ninety-five percent.
At sunrise, they shiver their flight muscles
and warm up for about an hour.
Then it's off to breakfast.
So would you rather shut down every night like a hummingbird,
or eat three times your weight every day?
