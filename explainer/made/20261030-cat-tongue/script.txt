Why does your cat's tongue feel like sandpaper?
It's covered in about 300 tiny hooks,
all pointing backwards.
Each hook is a spine called a papilla,
made of keratin,
the same stuff as your fingernails.
But the weirdest part?
The tip of every spine is hollow,
a tiny scoop, like a quill pen.
When a cat licks,
those scoops sip up saliva,
slide down through the fur,
and drop it onto the skin.
That pulls out dirt and tangles,
and as the spit dries,
it cools the cat down.
Scientists think grooming handles
up to a quarter of a cat's cooling.
No wonder cats spend up to a quarter
of their waking hours licking themselves.
Researchers built a hairbrush
copying the cat's tongue.
So would you brush your hair
with three hundred tiny hooks?
