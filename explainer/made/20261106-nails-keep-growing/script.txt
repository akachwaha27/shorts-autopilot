Your nails never stop growing,
but your arms stopped years ago.
So why does one keep going?
It starts with your bones.
Near each end of a long bone
sits a soft strip called a growth plate.
It keeps adding new bone,
so your arms get longer.
But by your late teens,
that strip turns to solid bone.
The plate closes,
and your arms stop for good.
Nails work differently.
Under your cuticle hides the nail matrix,
a tiny factory that never shuts down.
It keeps making new cells,
pushing the older ones forward.
Those cells harden and die,
which is why clipping doesn't hurt.
Fingernails grow about three millimeters a month,
so a whole new nail takes around six months.
So which grows faster:
your fingernails, or your toenails?
