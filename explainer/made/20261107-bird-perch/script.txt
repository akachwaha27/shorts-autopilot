A sleeping bird never falls off its branch.
You'd never guess why.
When a bird bends its legs,
tendons behind its ankles pull tight,
and that curls the toes around the twig.
In songbirds, tiny bumps on those tendons
catch on ridges, like a zip tie,
so the grip holds with little effort.
To let go, it just stands up.
But here's the twist.
When scientists filmed sleeping starlings,
their legs weren't bent enough to lock.
They were mostly balancing,
probably helped by a balance sensor
near their hips, not just their ears.
Many birds even sleep with half their brain,
one eye open for danger.
That's how perching birds,
over half of all bird species,
nap safely in the trees.
So when a bird dozes on a wire,
is it gripping, or just balancing?
