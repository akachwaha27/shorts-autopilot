Frogs actually swallow with their eyeballs.
You've just never noticed why.
Watch a frog gulp down a cricket,
and its eyes squeeze shut
and sink right into its head.
That's not a blink.
A frog's eye sockets are open underneath,
with no solid bone floor.
So muscles yank each eyeball down,
into the roof of its mouth.
Now those eyes press on the bug like two thumbs,
shoving it back toward the throat.
Why bother?
Frogs don't chew.
Their tiny teeth only grip,
so dinner goes down whole.
Scientists tested this on leopard frogs.
With those eye muscles switched off,
each cricket took 74 percent more swallows.
About four gulps instead of two.
So a frog's eyes spot the bug,
then help push it down.
Next time a frog blinks at you,
