Your shrimp was blue-gray two minutes ago.
Why is it pink now?
The pink was there the whole time.
Shrimp eat tiny algae
packed with a red pigment called astaxanthin.
But inside a living shrimp,
a protein grabs that pigment
and twists it out of shape.
And a twisted pigment looks blue-gray, not red.
Now add heat.
The protein unravels and lets go,
and the pigment snaps back to its true color.
Pink!
Lobsters and crabs do the exact same thing.
And that same pigment is why salmon are pink,
and it helps paint flamingos pink too.
They all get it from what they eat.
So the color wasn't made in the pan.
It was just hiding.
Be honest, did you think raw shrimp were pink?
