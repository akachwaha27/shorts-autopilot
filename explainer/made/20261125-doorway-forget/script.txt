Why do you forget why you walked into a room?
Blame the door.
In 2011, psychologist Gabriel Radvansky tested it.
People carried objects between tables, in virtual rooms.
Crossing one big room, they remembered better.
Walking through a doorway, they forgot more.
Your brain saves life in chapters, called events.
A doorway tells it: new scene.
So it files the last chapter away,
with your plan inside.
Weirdly, walking back into the first room didn't help.
A 2021 study found it hits hardest
when your mind is already juggling something else.
So what did a door make you forget today?
