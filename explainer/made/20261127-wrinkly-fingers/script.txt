Stop blaming the water.
Your nerves wrinkle your fingers on purpose.
For years, people blamed water soaking into the skin.
But since the 1930s, doctors noticed something odd.
If a finger's nerves are damaged,
that finger stays perfectly smooth.
So it isn't the water. It's you.
After a long soak, your nervous system squeezes
the tiny blood vessels under your fingertips.
The pads shrink, and the skin folds into ridges,
like the treads on a rain tire.
In 2013, scientists tested it with marbles.
With wrinkly fingers, people moved wet marbles
twelve percent faster.
Dry marbles? No difference at all.
So, are those soggy fingers,
or your built-in rain tires?
