Male seahorses get pregnant,
and you've never seen a weirder birth:
up to a thousand babies at once.
So why him, and not her?
After a twirly mating dance,
the female drops her eggs
into a pouch on the male's belly.
He seals them inside
and grows tiny blood vessels around them,
a lot like a placenta.
He feeds them oxygen and nutrients
and keeps the water just salty enough.
About two to four weeks later,
he goes into labor,
pumping and squeezing until the babies shoot out in a cloud.
Here's the clever bit.
While he's busy carrying,
she's already making her next batch of eggs.
Some dads give birth in the morning
and are pregnant again by evening.
More babies, way faster.
So would you trade jobs with a seahorse dad?
