Pill bugs roll into balls,
but you've never seen the real reason why.
First, they aren't bugs at all.
They're crustaceans,
cousins of crabs, shrimp and lobsters.
Count the legs. Fourteen, not six.
Their back is a row of hard, overlapping plates.
But their belly is soft,
with legs and tiny gills.
Yep, gills.
They breathe like their sea cousins,
so they have to stay damp.
Now poke one, or shake the ground.
It tucks its head, folds its tail,
and the plates lock into a perfect armoured ball.
A hungry ant just finds a smooth shell
with nothing soft to grab.
And inside, the ball traps moisture,
so those gills don't dry out.
Their cousins, sow bugs, can't roll up.
They just run for it.
So, armour or speed?
Which one would you pick?
