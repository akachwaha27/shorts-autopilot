A 500-kilo horse can sleep standing up,
and you'd never guess why it doesn't fall.
The trick is called the stay apparatus.
Tendons and ligaments brace its legs,
so the joints lock with almost no muscle effort.
In a back leg, the kneecap hooks
over a bump on the thigh bone,
like a latch clicking shut.
The other back leg gets to rest,
and every so often it swaps sides.
But there's a catch.
Dreaming sleep, called REM, relaxes every muscle,
and that unlocks the legs.
So for REM, a horse has to lie down,
about thirty to forty-five minutes a day,
often while a buddy stands guard.
Miss it for a week or so,
and its knees can buckle mid-nap.
So, could you sleep standing up,
or would you fall flat on your face?
