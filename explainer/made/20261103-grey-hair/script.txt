Your grey hairs start with stuck cells,
tiny ones, at your roots.
Hair gets its colour from melanin,
a pigment made by cells called melanocytes.
They sit at the bottom of each follicle,
and inject colour as the hair grows.
But those cells need replacing,
and that's the job of stem cells.
In mice, scientists found these stem cells commute,
moving up and down the follicle.
Moving is how they get the signal to make colour.
But as hair ages, more of them stop.
They get stuck in a spot called the bulge.
In plucked mouse hair, stuck cells jumped,
from fifteen percent to nearly fifty.
Stuck cells can't make new colour cells.
Less melanin means grey,
no melanin means white.
By your early sixties,
about ninety-one percent of people have some grey.
So if scientists could unstick those cells,
would you keep your grey, or go back?
