A snail never lifts its one foot.
Can you guess how it moves?
Put a snail on glass and look underneath.
Dark stripes ripple along the sole,
from the tail toward the head.
Those are waves of muscle,
squeezing and stretching the foot.
But the real trick is the slime.
Under the still parts of the foot,
it's thick and sticky, like glue.
Where a wave pushes on it,
it turns runny and slippery.
So each wave slides forward on liquid,
while the rest stays stuck down.
Glue, slide, glue, slide.
That grip lets a snail climb straight up a stem,
or hang under a leaf.
The catch? It's slow.
Roughly one millimetre a second.
So would you trade your two feet
for one slimy one?
