How does a mosquito find you in the pitch dark?
Your breath.
Only females hunt you,
because they need blood to make eggs.
Every time you breathe out,
you leave a trail of carbon dioxide.
Scientists think she can smell it
from up to fifty meters away,
about half a football field.
She follows that invisible trail.
Closer in, she picks up your body odor.
In daylight, she'd spot you from about fifteen meters,
but tonight, that's no help.
So here's her secret weapon.
Tiny pits on her antenna tips
sense the infrared heat your skin gives off,
from about seventy centimeters away.
In one lab study, adding that heat
doubled how hard mosquitoes searched.
Breath, then smell, then heat.
And she lands.
So tell me:
do mosquitoes always pick you, or someone else?
