Why can't you hear an owl
until it's three feet from you?
Most birds' wings churn the air into big swirls,
and those swirls are the whoosh you hear.
But an owl's front wing feathers have a comb,
tiny teeth along the edge.
They split the air into small, quiet streams.
Then a soft, ragged fringe on the back edge
and a velvety coat on top
hush whatever noise is left.
Big wings help too.
A barn owl can fly as slowly as two miles an hour,
and slower means quieter.
Its wings mute the high sounds above sixteen hundred hertz,
right where a mouse's hearing begins.
So the mouse never hears it coming,
but the owl hears the mouse just fine.
If an owl flew over you tonight,
do you think you'd hear it?
