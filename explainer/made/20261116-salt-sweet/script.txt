Salt makes your dessert sweeter.
You've probably never noticed why.
Bakers sneak it into cookies,
caramel and chocolate.
So what's salt really doing on your tongue?
Trick number one.
Most foods hide a little bitterness,
and sodium turns your bitter taste cells down,
like muting the loudest guy in the room.
With bitter quiet, sweet gets the spotlight.
Trick number two.
Scientists found your sweet taste cells have a tiny sugar door
called S G L T one.
It pulls sugar in together with sodium,
so a little salt may help those cells catch even more sweetness.
And you barely need any.
A pinch is about a sixteenth of a teaspoon,
around a third of a gram.
Add too much and it just tastes salty.
So when you salt a slice of watermelon,
are you making it saltier, or sweeter?
