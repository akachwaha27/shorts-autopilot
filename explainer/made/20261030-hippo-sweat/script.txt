Hippos sweat red goo,
and you'll never see one sunburned.
But why?
A hippo spends hours in the African sun,
with almost no hair to protect it.
So its skin fights back.
Glands under the skin
ooze a thick, clear goo.
Within a few minutes,
it turns bright red,
then a rusty orange-brown.
That's why people call it blood sweat.
But it's not blood,
and it's not really sweat.
The color comes from two pigments,
one red, one orange.
Together, they soak up ultraviolet light,
like built-in sunscreen.
And the red one does even more.
In lab tests,
it stopped nasty bacteria from growing.
Handy, when rivals leave scratches all over you.
So a hippo makes its own sunscreen,
and its own antibiotic.
If you could sweat any color,
which one would you pick?
