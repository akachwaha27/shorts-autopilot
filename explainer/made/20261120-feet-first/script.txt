Your feet grow before the rest of your body.
It's why teenagers can look like baby giraffes.
Big feet, long legs,
and a body still catching up.
So what's going on?
Inside your bones are growth plates,
soft strips of cartilage near each end.
They keep adding new bone,
until they harden into solid bone and close for good.
When puberty kicks in,
growth starts at the edges and works inward.
Hands and feet go first,
then your arms and legs,
and your torso grows last.
Small bones,
like the ones in your fingers and toes,
mature faster than the long bones in your legs.
That's why teens trip over their own feet.
Then, somewhere around 13 to 17,
the last growth plates close,
and the rest of you finally catches up.
So, did your feet show up before the rest of you?
