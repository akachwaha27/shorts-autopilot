Three hearts, blue blood,
and you'd never guess why one octopus needs 3.
Two of the hearts sit right by the gills.
Their only job is pushing blood through them,
so it can grab oxygen from the water.
The third heart is the big one.
It sends that fresh blood out to the whole body.
So why not just one?
Octopus blood carries oxygen with copper, not iron.
That copper is what makes it blue,
and it's worse at holding oxygen than ours.
So the octopus needs extra pumping power.
Now the weird part.
When an octopus swims,
that main body heart actually stops beating.
Swimming tires it out fast,
which is why octopuses would much rather crawl.
If you had three hearts,
what would you do with the spare one?
