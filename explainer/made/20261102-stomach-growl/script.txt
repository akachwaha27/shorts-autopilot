Your empty stomach growls every two hours until you eat.
But why won't it stop?
Your gut is one long muscle tube,
and its walls squeeze in waves.
That's called peristalsis.
They push food, liquid and gas along,
about three squeezes a minute in your stomach.
After a meal, food muffles the sound.
But empty? It's mostly gas and air,
so every squeeze echoes like a drum.
When you've been empty a while,
a big cleaning wave kicks in.
It sweeps leftover food and bacteria
down the whole gut.
It rumbles for ten to twenty minutes,
then comes back every one to two hours,
until you eat again.
The ancient Greeks called it borborygmi,
which means rumbling.
So that growl isn't just hunger.
It's your gut taking out the trash.
