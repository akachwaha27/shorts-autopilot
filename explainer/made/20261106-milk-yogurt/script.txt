Your yogurt is milk that two kinds of bacteria soured in about five hours.
First, the milk gets heated,
to around two hundred degrees Fahrenheit.
That changes its proteins,
so they can set together later.
Then it cools to about a hundred and ten,
and two kinds of bacteria get stirred in.
One looks like chains of tiny beads,
the other like little rods.
They only want one thing:
lactose, the sugar in milk.
For about five hours,
they eat it, and make lactic acid.
Then comes the magic number:
four point six.
At that pH, floating proteins clump,
into a soft, wobbly net.
That net traps the liquid,
and thin milk turns thick.
That tangy taste? That's the acid too.
So tell me,
would you still eat it, knowing bacteria made it?
