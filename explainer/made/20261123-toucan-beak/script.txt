A toucan's beak is secretly a radiator.
Here's why you never noticed.
That giant bill is a third of the bird's length,
but barely a twentieth of its weight.
It's mostly air.
Thin sheets of keratin, the stuff in your nails,
wrap a foam of tiny bone struts.
Now the clever part.
Under that shell runs a web of blood vessels.
When the toucan gets hot,
it pumps warm blood into the beak,
and the heat pours out into the air.
Scientists filmed it with a heat camera
and watched the beak light up.
When it's chilly, it shuts the flow off
and keeps that heat inside.
For short bursts,
almost all the heat it loses
goes out through that beak.
Be honest, would you trade your nose
for a radiator on your face?
