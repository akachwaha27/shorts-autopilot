You'd faint with your brain two meters up.
Giraffes never do. Why?
A giraffe swings its head from the water,
all the way up to the treetops,
and nothing happens.
First, a monster heart.
It weighs about eleven kilos,
with walls thick enough to pump
at twice your blood pressure.
Second, valves in the neck veins.
When the head drops down to drink,
they stop blood flooding back into the brain.
Third, scientists think the neck veins store blood,
and when the head swings up,
it rushes to the heart
for one extra-strong beat.
But all that pressure
would make anyone's legs swell,
so giraffes wear built-in compression socks:
super-tight skin around their legs.
So, if you had a giraffe's heart,
would you still get dizzy
with your brain two meters up?
