Why do crabs walk sideways?
You'd never guess it's their knees.
A crab's body is wide and flat,
perfect for squeezing under rocks.
But that shape stiffens its shoulder joints.
So the real bending happens one joint down,
a simple hinge, like your knee,
that only swings out to the side.
Eight walking legs, all hinged that way.
Going forward, a crab can only shuffle.
Sideways, those hinges swing wide,
and it zooms.
It can dart left or right in a flash,
so a hungry gull can't guess where it's going.
Scientists filmed fifty kinds of crabs.
Thirty-five of them walked sideways.
They think the trick showed up just once,
about two hundred million years ago.
So if your knees bent sideways,
where would you scuttle first?
