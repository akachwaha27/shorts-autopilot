Why does a banana get brown spots?
You'd never guess it's on purpose.
It starts with a gas called ethylene.
The banana makes it all by itself,
and it flips ripening on like a switch.
First, the green chlorophyll in the peel breaks down,
so the yellow underneath finally shows.
Inside, starch turns into sugar.
Sugar can climb from about 1 percent to around 17.
Then the peel starts getting old.
Little clusters of cells shut down on schedule,
and an enzyme called polyphenol oxidase
links chemicals in the peel into brown pigment.
That's your spot.
And here's the wild part.
Under ultraviolet light,
each spot glows with a bright blue ring,
from leftover bits of chlorophyll.
So more spots usually means a sweeter banana.
Which one do you grab:
green, yellow, or totally spotty?
