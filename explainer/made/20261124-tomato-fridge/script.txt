Your fridge is killing your tomatoes' flavor.
Most people never notice why.
A ripe tomato's taste isn't just sweet and sour.
It's hundreds of tiny smell molecules,
and your nose does most of the tasting.
Those smells are made by genes inside the fruit,
switched on while it sits somewhere warm.
But scientists kept tomatoes
at five degrees Celsius for a week.
The cold turned those genes way down.
The smell molecules dropped
by up to sixty-five percent!
The sugar and acid didn't change at all.
So it's still sweet,
but it tastes like wet cardboard.
Seventy-six taste testers noticed right away.
And a week in the cold
leaves marks on the fruit's DNA switches,
so warming it back up barely helps.
Keep ripe ones on the counter instead.
So, where are your tomatoes right now?
