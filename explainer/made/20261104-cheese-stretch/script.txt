Your pizza cheese hides a protein net,
you've never seen.
Long proteins called casein,
are tied together by tiny calcium bonds,
with fat and water stuck in the gaps.
Cold, that net is stiff.
Warm it up, and the fat melts first,
then the bonds loosen.
Now the strands slide past each other,
without letting go.
Mozzarella has an extra trick.
Makers dunk the curd in hot water,
around 70 to 80 degrees Celsius,
then pull and fold it like dough,
lining the proteins up in long fibers.
Old cheese is different.
As it ages, enzymes snip those strands,
so it melts, but it won't stretch.
Ricotta barely melts at all,
its proteins are locked with permanent bonds.
So a cheese pull is a net,
bending but not breaking.
Which cheese has the best pull on your pizza?
