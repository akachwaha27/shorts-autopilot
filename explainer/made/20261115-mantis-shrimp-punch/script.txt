A shrimp the size of your thumb
punches at 80 kilometers an hour,
hard enough to crack glass. How?
Meet the mantis shrimp.
It doesn't pinch with claws.
It swings a club folded under its head.
And the trick isn't muscle.
Big muscles squeeze a spring
shaped like a saddle,
kind of like a Pringles chip.
A tiny latch holds it locked.
Then, click, the latch lets go.
The club fires in under three thousandths of a second.
So fast the water in front of it boils into bubbles.
When those bubbles collapse,
they slam the shell a second time,
with a flash of light and heat.
One punch, two hits.
Snail shells don't stand a chance.
And when the club wears down?
It just molts and grows a fresh one.
So if it can crack glass,
what would you keep it in?
