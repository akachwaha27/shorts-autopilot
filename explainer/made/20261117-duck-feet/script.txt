Ducks stand barefoot on ice for hours,
and their feet never freeze.
Yours would. Why?
A duck's body runs hot,
around 41 degrees.
If that warm blood rushed into its feet,
the ice would suck the heat right out.
So inside each leg, there's a trick.
The artery carrying warm blood down
runs tight against the veins coming back up.
As they pass, the warm blood hands its heat
to the cold blood heading home.
Scientists call it countercurrent heat exchange.
So the feet only get blood that's barely warm,
just enough to dodge frostbite,
and the heat goes back into the body.
In one study, mallards at freezing
lost only about 5 percent of their body heat through their feet.
And duck feet are mostly bone and tendon,
with hardly any soft tissue to freeze.
Pretty genius, right?
So would you swap feet with a duck,
just for one winter?
