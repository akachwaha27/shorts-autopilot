A tick glues itself into your skin in thirty minutes,
and you never feel it.
First, two tiny scissor blades
snip a hole in your skin.
Then it slides in a spear called the hypostome,
covered in barbs that point backwards,
like a fishhook.
Once it's in, it won't slide back out.
Now the wild part.
Within minutes,
some ticks start oozing saliva
that hardens into cement,
a little cone of glue around the spear.
And that spit carries a painkiller,
so you don't notice a thing.
It can hang on for a week or more,
and some females swell up
to a hundred times their size.
When it's full,
it breaks free from its own glue in a minute or two,
and drops off.
So which grip is scarier,
the barbs or the glue?
