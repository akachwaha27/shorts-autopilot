You'd topple in seconds,
but a 500-kilo horse sleeps standing for hours.
The secret is a lock,
built right into its legs.
It's called the stay apparatus,
a web of tendons and ligaments
that holds the joints straight
with almost no muscle effort.
The back leg is even cleverer.
The kneecap slides up
and hooks over a ridge on the thighbone,
so the leg locks
like a kickstand.
The horse leans on that leg,
tips the other hoof,
and dozes.
If a predator shows up,
it's already on its feet.
But that's only light sleep.
To dream,
a horse has to lie down,
because its muscles go totally slack.
It needs about half an hour of that a day.
So would you rather lie down to dream,
or sleep standing up, like a horse?
